# Post-entry trade-management audit — 23 September 2026

## Verdict

The management engine is protective by design, and it has several independently evaluated exit paths. The clearest risk is not that every individual threshold is too tight. It is that the intended holding horizon is implicit: 5-minute and 15-minute trades can still receive 1-minute trailing and scalp-mode profit protection. A trade can also be marked both `runner_mode` and `scalp_lock_mode`, so runner activation does not always release the scalp policy.

This can make a longer-timeframe setup behave like a scalp, especially in RANGING/UNKNOWN or when the entry is tagged late/choppy. That is a credible design mismatch and the best first target for an isolated test. The evidence does not justify globally loosening stops, disabling the loss guards, or choosing new numeric thresholds. The historical trade sample is small and predates the latest fixes; the available walk-forward exit replay is negative and does not run the complete live management stack.

This was a read-only audit. No product code, settings, orders, positions, or runtime process were changed. The values below came from the workspace's loaded settings. No live trading process was verified, so they should not be treated as a confirmed running-process snapshot.

## What can act after entry

The current workspace configuration enables smart trailing and intelligence early exit. `adaptive_hold_enabled` is false. A 15-minute trade has a dedicated faster support/reversal check: it observes one-minute structure, but the normal path waits at least 15 seconds and requires a completed 1-minute reversal plus higher-timeframe or market-wide confirmation, durable corroboration, an eligible trade-state band, and adverse verified option-premium movement. The first 60 seconds use a separate fresh multi-source path; a catastrophic underlying shock still needs premium confirmation. This is not a blind 1-minute stop. See `scanner.py:22479-22827` and the call at `scanner.py:36402`.

Ordinary trailing uses the trade's own timeframe engine in a trending regime. In RANGING or UNKNOWN, `uses_tight_trailing()` instead proposes the prior completed 1-minute candle's low/high, even for a 5-minute or 15-minute position (`scanner.py:36591-36631`; `engine/market_regime.py:100-123`). This is the strongest direct source of a timeframe mismatch. VOLATILE is excluded from that 1-minute trail.

The profit lock is a tiered protective stop, not an exit at its arm. The current settings arm it from a combination of an absolute floor, initial risk, position cost, and regime. The ordinary non-scalp arm uses at least ₹250 and 0.20R; a normal scalp arm uses at least ₹500 and 0.50R. A 15-minute choppy-scalp entry uses the special ₹250/0.20R arm. Under ₹1,000 per ladder basis, the standard retained-peak ratio is 50%, with a 38% ratio for VOLATILE and BREAKOUT regimes; higher tiers retain more, up to the configured 90% ceiling. `profit_lock_per_lot_basis` is enabled, so the tier is selected per lot. See `scanner.py:17609-17681`, `scanner.py:17993-18123`, and `config/settings.py:1274-1343`.

Several additional protections can act on a fading winner: breakeven protection arms after 60 seconds and a peak of at least the larger of the scaled ₹100 threshold or 0.40R, then exits at a gross P&L floor of ₹80 (scaled); peak-threat requires a meaningful peak and adverse evidence with a five-second confirmation; the giveback guard keeps at least 50% of peak in its applicable contexts; live reversal can exit after a 15% fade when the trade's own regime or multiple peer indices oppose it. A nontrend session phase applies the giveback guard to all trades at a lower arm. Confirmed trend runners are exempt from that nontrend giveback rule, outside the EOD and unconfirmed-repaint cases. `peak_giveback_cap_enabled` is false, so its separate fixed-rupee cap is not active. Relevant code is at `scanner.py:18167-18315`, `scanner.py:19173-19978`, and `scanner.py:19979-20124`.

Loss-side rules are also layered, but have conditions. The early-loss cut waits at least 15 seconds, observes the round-trip cost floor, defers losses on trades that already proved a peak to the profit-protection path, and normally cuts at 0.80R; 0.55R/0.35R cuts require stronger reversal evidence. The ₹1,000 option/₹1,500 futures loss-band values are arming regions, not unconditional stops: severity, freefall, slow-bleed, or the larger force-loss condition must also qualify. Hard stop and observed contract-touch protection remain authoritative. `scanner.py:23147-23350`, `scanner.py:23584-23760`, and `trading/trade_manager.py:7106-7179`.

Other exits include low-gain protection, the major-win guard, stagnation after 20 minutes (with up to 10 minutes of volatility-adjusted runway), EOD loss minimization, and the 15:15 force exit with a 14-minute EOD protection window. They are conditional and evaluated in a first-eligible-exit sequence; they do not all fire on every trade. The scanner now passes its early-loss verdict to TradeManager for that update only, and the 23 September repair tests verify that a deferral cannot mask an observed hard-stop touch. See `scanner.py:37155-37359` and `reports/workflow_fix_verification_20260923.md`.

## Where the “scalper” behavior enters

The `scalp_lock_mode` flag is broader than a short holding-period contract. The location gate sets it for anything that is not a qualifying own-timeframe, 15-minute-aligned trend pullback (`scanner.py:28103-28118`). Late entries and the special 15-minute choppy setup can force it on (`scanner.py:20509-20525`). Those choices may be intentional for risk control, but they make a 5-minute or 15-minute source timeframe insufficient to predict how long the engine will hold.

Runner mode is a separate flag. T1/big-move runner activation sets `runner_mode` and removes the target, but does not generally clear `scalp_lock_mode` (`scanner.py:36724-36782`). The later profit-lock calculation still receives the scalp flag (`scanner.py:36931-36938`). Only the one-way-cascade runner path explicitly clears it (`scanner.py:36644-36653`). This permits a runner stop and scalp-oriented lock eligibility to coexist. The historical ledger includes examples with both flags set, although those trades are from an earlier build and cannot prove the current policy's outcomes.

The breakeven label also deserves a cost check. It tests a gross ₹80 floor, while actual round-trip charges and spread can exceed that amount. The code itself notes that a breakeven exit can still be net negative after costs (`scanner.py:18191-18201`). The current arm's 0.40R/60-second conditions are not universally too tight, but the fixed gross floor does not guarantee net breakeven.

This replay is not a complete reproduction of the current live exit stack and predates the latest fixes. The 23 September focused repair selection passed 187 tests, which verifies decision mechanics and precedence, not a trading edge.

The separate accepted/rejected shadow outcomes in `workflow_evidence_20260923.json` are fixed-horizon entry outcomes, not management-stack simulations. They vary by horizon and are not randomized or broker fills. They cannot answer whether a specific exit rule helped or hurt.

## Recommended next step

Do not globally loosen the floors or remove the 15-minute safety check. First make the position's management intent explicit and immutable at fill: `SCALP`, `5min`, or `15min`, with runner mode as a defined transition. For 5-minute/15-minute intent, test whether the prior-1-minute trail and scalp lock should remain eligible; retain hard stops, quote validity, EOD square-off, and a high-bar, premium-confirmed catastrophe response.

Run paired, one-change-at-a-time shadow replays on identical accepted entries and exact option contracts. Record each guard's would-exit time, observed premium, current and peak P&L, initial R, spread/charges, MFE/MAE, and continuation at 5/15/30 minutes after the candidate exit. Split by timeframe, regime, index, and scalp/runner state. Use chronological holdout and the existing certification floor of at least 60 sessions; require positive after-cost expectancy, profit factor above 1, and acceptable drawdown before deploying numeric threshold changes. Separately test the ₹80 gross breakeven floor against real costs.

The first two policy ablations to measure are (1) removing the 1-minute trailing proposal for longer-horizon positions in RANGING/UNKNOWN while leaving the initial and higher-timeframe stops in place, and (2) ensuring runner activation actually selects runner breathing rules instead of retaining a scalp lock. These isolate the strongest horizon conflicts without weakening hard-risk controls.
